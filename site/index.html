Scene 01 — Origin

Design Goals

Macha is not an NPC that can chat. It is a standard skeleton for NPCs that remember, stay themselves, and can be tested.

We start from the player, not from the model. What breaks immersion is rarely a weak answer — it is an NPC that forgets, contradicts itself, or has no reason to refuse.

So the first deliverable is not a game. It is a spec, a reference implementation, and a conformance suite small enough for one or two people to finish.

  • MemoryExperiences, relationships and causality that survive across sessions.
  • ArchitectureAn extensible, modular architecture instead of piled-up prompts.
  • CharacterA stable, believable, evolving persona for every NPC.
  • Standard, not product — open, engine-agnostic and model-agnostic.
  • Measurable — memory, character consistency, latency, cost and observability are all testable.
  • Swappable — change the model or the memory backend and the conformance tests still pass.
  • Finishable — 1–2 developers, a few weeks, no engine plugin required.

Scene 02 — Horizon

Industry Goals & Vision

Redefine the relationship between player, NPC and world — and make that definition public.

Today every studio re-invents NPC cognition from scratch. There is no shared vocabulary for what a game agent is, and no shared way to prove one works.

Macha aims to be that vocabulary: a thin, open standard that any engine and any model can implement, with evidence published in the open.

  • A shared vocabulary: what a game agent is, and what it must guarantee.
  • A semi-permeable membrane: the world shapes the NPC, the NPC keeps its own interpretation.
  • One standard, many worlds — the same interfaces across engines and models.
  • Public evidence: experiments, results and failures published in the open.

We are not trying to prove we can build a complex world. We are trying to prove what kind of relationship should exist between a player and an NPC — and why existing technology has never satisfied it.
