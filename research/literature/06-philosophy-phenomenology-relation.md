## What must be known (hard questions)
1. The other-minds problem in its artificial form — what behavioral consistency sustains the intentional stance.
2. Phenomenology of being-seen / being-remembered / being-changed — and what destroys each.
3. Fictionality (Walton's make-believe, Iser's blanks) in open generative systems — why a book survives on blanks but a generative NPC dies on violating history.

## Findings

### 1. The other-minds problem in its artificial form
- **Dennett's intentional stance is a predictive strategy, not a proof of inner life.** After the physical and design stances, we attribute beliefs/desires and predict rational behavior; much human-behavior prediction appears to involve such attribution.
  [<SEP — Belief>](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/belief/)
- **Operative steps:** treat the object as a rational agent; figure out the beliefs it ought to have given its place and purpose; figure out the desires it ought to have; predict rational action toward its goals.
  [<bliptext — intentional stance>](https://bliptext.com/articles/intentional-stance) [RE-VERIFY: replace with Dennett 1987 primary / SEP]
- **The criterion is predictive utility, not truth about inner states:** when the prediction "the system behaves as it should, given the propositional attitudes it should have, given its environment" succeeds, the system is intentional — "there is nothing more to having a propositional attitude than this" (Dennett's instrumentalist claim).
  [<SEP — Mental Representation>](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2024/entries/mental-representation/)
- **p-zombies reactivate the other-minds problem:** a philosophical zombie is physically identical but lacks conscious experience; zombies behave just like us. The scenario challenges physicalism (if zombies are conceivable, consciousness is not entailed by physical facts alone). For Macha: an NPC might behave relationally while lacking genuine relational state, and the player would still project relation into it.
  [<SEP — Zombies>](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/zombies/)
- **The other-minds problem is structurally the same as the NPC problem:** "how do I know that other beings exist who have thoughts, feelings and other mental attributes?" — with a Wittgensteinian line that the real problem may be conceptual (why we think there are other thinking beings at all).
  [<SEP — Other Minds>](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/other-minds/index.html)
- **Simulation theory is a partial answer, not a closure:** folk-psychology-as-simulation ("putting ourselves in their shoes") explains how we represent others' states but does not prove their inner states.
  [<SEP — Folk Psychology as Mental Simulation>](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/folkpsych-simulation/)
  - **Consume:** behavioral consistency CAN sustain the intentional stance; that is the strongest available guarantee. It does not prove inner life — Macha must not claim it does.

### 2. Phenomenology of being-seen / being-remembered / being-changed

**A. Being-seen / the gaze**
- **Sartre: the Look objectifies the subject.** In *Being and Nothingness*, being subject to the look of another makes us a "transcendence transcended" — existential objectification, judgment, definition as an object in the world.
  [<SEP — Sartre>](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/) [<Tucker, "Eyes that Bind">](https://aporia.byu.edu/pdfs/tucker-eyes_that_bind.pdf) [<Sartre Studies — the Look as call to freedom>](https://www.berghahnjournals.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/sartre-studies/28/2/ssi280206.pdf)
- **The voyeur case:** hearing footsteps behind him, the voyeur experiences himself as objectified by an Other — visible, embodied, ashamed.
  [<Cambridge — Being and Nothingness, bad faith>](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/AE7694636E1A4AB83B339FC2A76CFC6F/9781139020206c8_p196-229_CBO.pdf/bad-faith-in-human-life-being-and-nothingness.pdf)
  - **Computational correlate:** the NPC must maintain a player-state record that persists, updates, and causally shapes subsequent behavior — the player must be *attended to*.
  - **What destroys it:** discarding the player-state record after each interaction, or visible responses not causally shaped by it.

**B. Being-remembered / narrative identity**
- **Ricoeur: identity is narrative.** The kind of identity a person has (idem- and ipse-identity) is a narrative identity; it bridges sameness and selfhood across past/present/future.
  [<SEP — Paul Ricoeur>](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2014/entries/ricoeur/) [<Vessey — Oneself as Another>](http://www.davevessey.com/Vessey_Ricoeur.html)
- **Being recognized by another is constitutive of identity:** self-knowledge comes only through relation to the world and life with and among others.
  [<SEP — Paul Ricoeur>](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2014/entries/ricoeur/)
  - **Computational correlate:** the NPC must carry a durable, queryable history of past interactions/decisions; prior player choices must become part of future NPC responses.
  - **What destroys it:** memory-less resets, stateless NPCs, past actions with no causal impact on future behavior — if the NPC cannot narrate the shared history, the player is not remembered.

**C. Being-changed / independence — Buber's I-Thou vs I-It**
- **Buber: I-It reduces the other to object.** I-It is the root of all subject-object relations — everything (and ultimately itself) is instrumental, isolated, manipulable. I-Thou establishes the world of relation.
  [<SEP — Martin Buber>](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/) [<Rug textbook — I-It vs I-You>](https://opentextbooks.rug.nl/philosophymigration/chapter/buber/) [<Gradesaver — I and Thou>](https://www.gradesaver.com/i-and-thou/study-guide/quotes) — the latter two are secondary. [RE-VERIFY: cite Buber's *I and Thou* primary]
  - **Computational correlate:** the NPC must be able to update its own state in ways not fully predetermined by player input; the player's actions change the NPC, and the NPC can change the player's future options/beliefs.
  - **What destroys it:** fixed scripts, predefined dialogue trees, stateless responses, player input selecting only predetermined branches — the NPC remains an It.

**D. Levinas: the face of the Other as ethical summons**
- **Levinas: ethics is first philosophy.** The encounter with another person unfolds at a precognitive level through embodied sensibility.
  [<SEP — Emmanuel Levinas>](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/levinas/)
- **The face commands and summons:** "Naked and defenseless, the face signifies, with or without words, 'Do not kill me'"; it "opposes a passive resistance to our desire for mastery wherein our freedom asserts its sovereignty."
  [<SEP — Emmanuel Levinas>](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/levinas/)
  - **Computational correlate for independence:** the NPC must contain something like refusal, surprise, rejection, or an autonomous goal that can conflict with the player's preferred script — the NPC must be able to say "no," or be structured so the player cannot exhaustively control its future.
  - **What destroys it:** any system where the player's intended action is always accommodated, refusal is impossible, and NPC behavior is fully determined by player input.

### 3. Fictionality — Walton's make-believe & Iser's blanks, and the generative limit
- **Walton: props are generators of fictional truths.** A prop (doll, canvas, text, stump) prescribes imaginings by generating fictional truths; prop-oriented make-believe is make-believe utilized to understand props.
  [<SEP — Imagination>](https://plato.stanford.edu/ENTRiES/imagination/) [<Laur — make-believe>](http://marilaur.info/makebelieve.pdf) [<PhilPeople — Walton>](https://philpeople.org/profiles/kendall-walton/publications?order=viewings) — the latter two are secondary. [RE-VERIFY: cite Walton's *Mimesis as Make-Believe* primary]
- **Iser: the blank (Leerstelle).** Blanks within texts are "the blanks which the reader is to fill in," guiding the reader to adopt a position; zones of indeterminacy that require reader participation.
  [<Margins Journal — Leerstelle in Coetzee's Foe>](https://marginsjournal.wixsite.com/margins/post/1-1-yoong-the-agency-of-silence) [<Benjamins — reception et traduction>](https://www.benjamins.com/online/hts/articles/rec1.fr) — secondary. [RE-VERIFY: cite Iser's *The Act of Reading* primary]
- **Interactivity changes the fictionality stakes:** Walton's theory seems applicable to videogames (works as props; prop-oriented make-believe generates fictional truths), though Walton never mentions videogames.
  [<Fictionally Flipping Tetrominoes>](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/a02c/6b7b2ec08bc0220ad79b65ce83ba26835994.pdf)
- **Coleridge's "willing suspension of disbelief":** "that willing suspension of disbelief for the moment, which constitutes poetic faith"; requires "human interest and a semblance of truth" so the reader suspends judgment about implausibility.
  [<Gradesaver — Coleridge>](https://www.gradesaver.com/coleridges-poems/q-and-a/explain-coleridges-phrase-willing-suspension-of-disbelief-257325) [<CliffsNotes>](https://www.cliffsnotes.com/study-notes/28519776) — both secondary. [RE-VERIFY: cite Coleridge's *Biographia Literaria* primary]
  - **The generative limit (Macha's thesis):** a BOOK survives on blanks because the reader fills them and the text never contradicts itself; a GENERATIVE NPC dies when it violates established history because the prop (the remembered fiction) is damaged and the game of make-believe collapses. The NPC's remembered history is a prop that prescribes future imaginings — violating it breaks the fiction; preserving it sustains it.
  - **What destroys it:** NPC contradicting what it previously remembered; player actions repeatedly overwritten without trace.

## The sharpest objection, formalized + Macha's rebuttal

**Objection.** If NPCs are always experienced through the player's intentional stance, simulation, make-believe, and projective reading, then no computational architecture can guarantee a genuine relational state. An NPC might reproduce the behavioral patterns of being-seen/being-remembered/being-changed while lacking any actual independence, memory, or responsiveness. The p-zombie worry shows the threat is not just missing conscious experience, but that functionally similar systems could behave relationally without the relevant property.

**Macha's strongest rebuttal.** Computation cannot guarantee inner mental life in the Cartesian sense, but Dennett's intentional stance guarantees something Macha can claim: if the NPC is built so that player actions *causally change* the NPC's durable state, and the NPC's subsequent behavior is *nontrivially determined* by that updated state, then treating the NPC as intentional is rational. This is weaker than inner life, stronger than empty prop. Formalizable as:

> **NPC-Relational-Sufficiency:** If a computational system maintains a persistent relational state — a history of player interactions, an updated memory trace, and state transitions responsive to the player — then the intentional stance is rational toward that NPC, and the player has grounds to treat it as a relational object rather than a mere scripted It.

**Remaining risk.** If the relational state is only cached locally and never fed back into decision-making, the NPC may satisfy projection while failing Buber's I-Thou, Levinas's independent Other, or Ricoeur's narrative identity. The rebuttal therefore *requires* causal feedback of R into behavior — which is exactly Macha's architectural claim.

## Formal/operational takeaways for Macha
1. **R must be a first-class persistent object** — store and update a relational record (who the player is, what they did, how the NPC changed, what the NPC now believes).
2. **Player actions must be computationally irreversible** — history the player cannot simply undo (being-changed requires it).
3. **NPCs must be able to refuse or surprise** — goals/preferences/constraints that sometimes conflict with the player's desired outcome (Buber's I-It, Levinas's face).
4. **NPC memory must be causally effective** — past interactions change future behavior, not merely displayed as exposition (being-remembered).
5. **Being-seen requires a visible trace of attention** — acknowledgment, name use, reaction to history, memory-driven dialogue (not just a processed player ID).
6. **Fictional continuity is a relational constraint** — the NPC's remembered history is a Waltonian prop; violating it damages make-believe, preserving it sustains it.

## Pitfalls / avoid
- Don't confuse the intentional stance with mental life — it proves rational prediction strategy, not feelings.
- Don't equate visibility with memory — being-seen becomes being-remembered only when future behavior is influenced.
- Don't overclaim computational independence — the operational claim is autonomous state updates + non-preprogrammed conflicts, not free will.
- Don't treat blanks as universally good — generative NPC fiction depends on persistent props; underdetermination can break continuity, rigidity makes the NPC an It.
- Don't treat all NPC memory as relational memory — only facts that change attitude/behavior/identity relative to the player count.
