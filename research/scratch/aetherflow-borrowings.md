# What to Borrow from AetherFlow (Paper Perspective)

> Perspective: As a top-tier academic reviewer/scholar, identify which AetherFlow designs are worth entering our paper.
> Primary constraint: We are writing a paper now, not building an engine. Therefore, only borrow concepts that strengthen the theoretical contribution; do not borrow piles of engineering jargon.

---

## I. Screening Criteria

Ask only three questions:

1. Does it help us answer "what does the player desire in a game agent?"
2. Can it explain "why existing agents break immersion"?
3. Can it be translated into a verifiable paper argument, rather than being an engineering module?

Anything that fails these three questions, no matter how clever it is in engineering, should not enter the paper for now.

---

## II. Five Concepts Worth Borrowing

### 1. The Player as an "Entropy Source": the World Is Thrown Off Balance by the Player

**AetherFlow's original words**: The player is the only variable that can break the macro balance; the world does not exist to make the player comfortable, but will change its trajectory because of the player.

**Paper value**: This directly supports our conclusion that "the world responds to the player." It gives us a powerful formulation:

> The player does not need the world to be forever stable; the player needs the world to lose its old balance because of their own actions, and produce new consequences.

**Placement**: Can serve as the theoretical basis for the "can be changed" dimension in "the necessity of authenticity" or "the relationship gap."

---

### 2. Information Parallax: Different Parts of the World Perceive the Player Differently

**AetherFlow's original words**: The player is a wanted criminal in country A and a hero in country B; information diffusion has delays and blind spots, which the player can exploit to create drama.

**Paper value**: It turns the abstract concepts of "worldliness" and "being seen" into experienceable mechanics:

> Authenticity does not come from omniscience, but from information gaps. The NPC only knows what it should know, so the player can play different versions of themselves.

**Placement**: Can be used to develop the two dimensions "it sees me" and "it is connected to the world," and is also the best illustration of "the world is not an omniscient database."

---

### 3. Memory Is "Dehydration," Not "Saving"

**AetherFlow's original words**: MFlow does not store raw dialogue, but distills conversation into relationship deltas, emotional crystals, and fact cores.

**Paper value**: It supports our core point about "it remembers me":

> The player needs the NPC to remember what matters in the relationship, not to remember everything. Forgetting and bias are not flaws, but the prerequisite for memory to feel alive.

**Placement**: Put into the memory dimension of the "relationship gap," or as a counterexample in "the necessity of authenticity": making NPC memory a database is precisely why immersion breaks.

---

### 4. Causal Boomerang: the Player's Tiny Past Actions Return Dramatically from Afar

**AetherFlow's original words**: The beggar the player rejected early on eventually becomes the leader of the late-game resistance through long-term macro evolution, returning to confront the player.

**Paper value**: This is the ultimate expression of "can be changed" and "leaves consequences." It shows:

> What the world responds to is not "instant feedback," but "long-cycle causal continuity." The player needs to feel that their actions accumulate over time, rather than being reset to zero when the quest ends.

**Placement**: Can be used in the "tension system" or "the criterion of authenticity" to argue that authenticity is not instant reaction but temporal depth.

---

### 5. The Macro/Micro "Presence" Distinction: the World Outside the View vs. the Encounter Within the View

**AetherFlow's original words**: NPCs are low-power data outside the field of view, and are only activated by the LLM within the field of view.

**Paper value**: Stripping away the engineering implementation, it raises an important experiential question:

> The NPC's sense of life comes both from "its reaction when it appears before me" and from "the sense that it is still living when it is not in front of me."

This corresponds to our two dimensions of "it exceeds me" and "worldliness."

**But caution is required**: If handled poorly, this "absence means nonexistence" can undermine individual consistency. In the paper it should be discussed as a tension, not directly endorsed.

---

## III. AetherFlow Content Not Recommended for the Paper

### 1. The Name "CeaserChain" and the "Wave Function" Packaging

It is essentially a dynamic Markov state container. Acceptable as an engineering component, but as a paper concept it is too specific, too named, and easily triggers pseudoscience associations.

### 2. The Physical Metaphor of the "Four Fundamental Fields"

Already rejected by AetherFlow itself. No need to introduce it into the paper.

### 3. "Subjective Collapse" as a Complete Worldview

If the world collapses entirely around the player, it contradicts our conclusion that "the NPC has boundaries / does not revolve entirely around the player." It can only be discussed as an "extreme direction," not as a paper claim.

---

## IV. How These Borrowings Enter the Paper Structure

| Borrowed Concept | Which Part of the Paper It Enters | What Argumentative Function It Serves |
|---|---|---|
| Player as entropy source | The necessity of authenticity / relationship gap | Argue that "the world responds to the player" is not pandering, but imbalance and consequence |
| Information parallax | Relationship gap: seeing + worldliness | Argue that authenticity comes from information gaps, not omniscience |
| Memory dehydration | Relationship gap: remembering | Argue that memory is purposeful forgetting and bias |
| Causal boomerang | Tension system / criterion of authenticity | Argue that long-cycle causal continuity is the source of authenticity |
| Macro/micro presence distinction | Relationship gap: exceeding me + worldliness | Argue that the NPC must leave "traces of existence" when the player is absent |

---

## V. Suggested Formulation in the Paper

Do not write "AetherFlow designed a macro simulation layer," but abstract it into our own theoretical language:

> The player's actions must be able to travel across space and time and return to the player in a non-uniform, non-omniscient way; the NPC's memory must be selective and positional; the world must leave "traces of having existed" when the player is absent.

This way, AetherFlow turns from "someone else's engine design" into "empirical material supporting our arguments."

---

## VI. Conclusion

What is worth borrowing from AetherFlow is not its architecture, but the four experiential insights behind it:

1. The player is a disturbance source to the world;
2. Authenticity comes from information gaps;
3. Memory is dehydration;
4. Causality boomerangs.

These four insights can directly enter the "necessity of authenticity" and "relationship gap" sections of our paper, but they must be reformulated as a theory of player relationships, not cited as engineering solutions.
